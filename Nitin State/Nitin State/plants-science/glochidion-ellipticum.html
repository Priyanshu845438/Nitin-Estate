Bhoma

Glochidion ellipticum

Introduction

Bhoma (Glochidium ellipticum) is a plant native to certain regions, particularly prominent in areas like Assam, where it is locally known as Bhoma. Belonging to the Phyllanthaceae family, it is also recognized by names such as Assam Leaf-Flower and Chendana. This plant holds cultural significance in its native regions and has garnered attention for its potential medicinal properties.

Botanical Name Glochidion ellipticum
Kingdom Plantae
Synonyms Phyllanthus assamicus, Glochidion malabaricum
Order Malpighiales
Family  Phyllanthaceae (Amla family)
Species ellipticum
Habit Tree
Class Magnoliopsida (dicotyledons)
Genus Glochidion
Range India, Bangladesh, Myanmar

Nutritional Value

Bhoma exhibits diverse medicinal potential, with various parts of the plant utilized in traditional remedies. Across different regions, natives incorporate different plant parts such as the bark, leaves, and flowers into herbal formulations. The plant's nutritional value is predominantly attributed to its traditional medicinal uses, with specific applications varying depending on local practices and cultural beliefs.


Properties

Bhoma is characterized by its unique features typical of the Phyllanthaceae family. The plant typically grows as a perennial herb, featuring elliptical leaves and small flowers. Its distinct appearance and growth habits contribute to its recognition and utilization in various traditional practices.


Side Effects

Limited information is available regarding the potential side effects or safety considerations associated with Bhoma . As with any herbal remedy, caution should be exercised, particularly if using it for medicinal purposes. Individuals with known allergies or sensitivities should approach its use with care and seek guidance from healthcare professionals if necessary.


Uses & Effectiveness

Traditional uses of Bhoma vary across regions, with different parts of the plant employed for various purposes. In some areas, it is used for its purported medicinal properties, while in others, it may have cultural or ceremonial significance. However, scientific evidence regarding its effectiveness and specific uses remains limited, necessitating further research and exploration.


Precautions to Take

Given the limited information available, caution is advised when using Bhoma , especially during pregnancy, breastfeeding, or in individuals with underlying health conditions.
